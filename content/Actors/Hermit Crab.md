# Hermit Crab

## Role

Hermit Crab is an Anchored concept for borrowed protection, exposed softness,
and identities that require a found shell.

Use this concept for someone who survives by occupying systems, homes, titles,
or bodies they did not build.

## Natural Model

Hermit crabs have soft, vulnerable abdomens and use empty shells for protection.
As they grow, they must find new shells. A shell can be shelter, status, limit,
and trap at the same time.

Many hermit crabs are careful, opportunistic, and socially alert around the
availability of better shelter.

This makes Hermit Crab a figure of:

- borrowed armor
- growth that requires relocation
- vulnerability hidden inside practicality
- shelter as identity
- survival through reuse

## Campaign Use

Hermit Crab should represent the uneasy ethics of inhabiting what someone else
left behind.

They may be tied to abandoned homes, obsolete offices, inherited titles, old
ritual roles, or bodies of law that no longer fit.

Use Hermit Crab when the campaign needs to ask:

- what is ownership when survival requires borrowing
- when protection becomes confinement
- whether an old role can be outgrown without leaving someone exposed

## Table Motifs

- keys on a ring that clearly belonged to different people
- a rented office full of inherited furniture
- a person checking exits and available cover
- shells, helmets, lockers, borrowed uniforms
- sudden anxiety when a protective structure is damaged
