# Shirasu

## Role

Shirasu is an Anchored concept for tiny lives gathered in numbers, local food,
and the fragile boundary between abundance and consumption.

Use this concept for an Anchored tied to smallness, seasonality, and being
counted only as a mass.

## Natural Model

Shirasu are tiny juvenile fish commonly eaten in coastal Japan. They are pale,
delicate, seasonal, and often encountered as a collective ingredient rather than
as individual animals.

They connect sea life, local economy, domestic meals, and the ordinary intimacy
of eating what the coast provides.

This makes Shirasu a figure of:

- small lives made collective
- abundance that depends on timing
- local appetite and local memory
- invisibility through quantity
- childhood, food, and mortality sitting close together

## Campaign Use

Shirasu should represent the people and traces too small for official history.

They may be tied to missing names, erased minor witnesses, school lunches,
market stalls, family meals, or records that count groups but not individuals.

Use Shirasu when the campaign needs:

- tenderness around ordinary coastal life
- a reminder that small losses accumulate
- clues hidden in food routes, markets, or seasonal routines

## Table Motifs

- market trays, rice bowls, lunch boxes
- pale crowds in shallow water
- ledgers that count weight instead of names
- children remembering what adults overlook
- a voice that insists small things are still lives
