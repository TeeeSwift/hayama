# Drifted Families and Daimon

## Purpose

This note defines old Drifted family structures and the local power represented
by Daimon. These groups should add social density, factional ambiguity, and
non-Anchored sources of old knowledge.

The goal is not to make every Drifted person part of a family faction. Instead,
these families show that some Drifted lineages have preserved memory, ritual,
and partial power across generations despite their divinity becoming displaced or
misaligned.

## Drifted Families

Most Drifted are individuals, loose networks, or recent inheritors of unstable
or misplaced divinity. Drifted families are different.

A Drifted family is a lineage that has retained some combination of:

- memory of its original divinity
- inherited rituals
- family records
- partial usable powers
- old grudges
- taboos around names, places, offerings, or marriage
- stories about what the Anchored did or failed to prevent
- strategies for surviving with damaged divinity

They are not necessarily unified with [[Actors/The Drifted|The Drifted]] as a
political faction. Some may support Drifted agitation, some may avoid it, and
some may use it only when useful.

## The Tsunodas

The Tsunodas are a Drifted family who have preserved unusually clear memory of
their divine lineage.

### Story Function

The Tsunodas embody inherited memory, grievance, and continuity after spiritual
displacement.

They can say:

> We remember what was taken from us.

They are useful when the story needs:

- a non-Anchored account of old divine history
- preserved memory that resists reaping distortions
- a family that treats lost divinity as identity rather than mere power
- pressure on [[Actors/Player Characters/Aizawa Marin|Marin]] as someone carrying
  displaced divinity
- an emotionally convincing critique of the Barrier and the Anchored

### Partial Powers

The Tsunodas should not have full, clean divinity. Their power is damaged but
cultivated.

Possible expressions:

- sensing divine residue in people, objects, or locations
- preserving names and memories through ritual recitation
- recognizing the original source of a displaced blessing
- resisting memory softening during reaping events
- binding a small manifestation briefly by naming what it used to be
- reading inherited records that become illegible to outsiders
- calling back a fragment of ancestral power at personal cost

### Attitude Toward Other Groups

- Anchored: see them as suppressors, thieves, jailers, or cowards depending on
  the speaker
- Drifted: kin by condition, though not always by politics
- Suzukis: useful but too willing to compromise
- Daimon: necessary, suspicious, and probably owed favors
- Civic authority: often naive tools of deeper systems
- Marin: possible proof, warning, heir, theft, or opportunity

### Symbolism

The Tsunodas symbolize memory as resistance.

They are what happens when a wound becomes genealogy.

## The Suzukis

The Suzukis are a Drifted family who retained functional power and adapted it
into ordinary local life.

They are less concerned with restoring a pure lost form and more concerned with
surviving, prospering, and keeping doors open.

### Story Function

The Suzukis embody adaptation, leverage, and the usefulness of imperfect
inheritance.

They can say:

> Loss is only loss if you refuse to use what remains.

They are useful when the story needs:

- Drifted power that is practical rather than tragic
- an access broker who can help the party for a price
- a family that understands land, thresholds, favors, and obligations
- a morally ambiguous ally embedded in everyday Hayama
- contrast with the Tsunodas' memory-proud conservatism

### Partial Powers

The Suzukis' powers should feel social, spatial, contractual, and threshold-based.

Possible expressions:

- knowing when someone crosses a property threshold they control
- sensing debts, favors, invitations, and broken promises
- making small bargains stick unnaturally well
- blessing or spoiling a transaction
- finding side doors, service corridors, delivery routes, and forgotten access
  points
- passing unnoticed where they have been invited
- turning hospitality into temporary protection
- identifying who has permission to be somewhere, even when the law disagrees

### Attitude Toward Other Groups

- Anchored: old business partners, old enemies, or both
- Drifted: useful banner, but too emotionally volatile when organized badly
- Tsunodas: respected, inconvenient, too attached to purity and grievance
- Daimon: family asset, inheritance, burden, and power base
- Civic authority: a system to work with, work around, or quietly own pieces of
- Marin: someone whose misalignment may be dangerous but negotiable

### Symbolism

The Suzukis symbolize adaptation as survival.

They are what happens when a wound becomes infrastructure.

## Daimon

Daimon is a local liquor store and quiet access power in Hayama.

On the surface, it is ordinary: alcohol, snacks, cigarettes, festival supplies,
local gossip, deliveries, and seasonal gift boxes.

Underneath, Daimon is a hidden boss of local logistics, land access, favors, and
keys.

### Surface Role

Daimon can appear as:

- a neighborhood liquor store
- a delivery van at strange locations
- a supplier for festivals, shrines, restaurants, and civic events
- a place where workers, officials, fishers, and old families all pass through
- a local institution everyone treats as normal

### Hidden Role

Daimon owns, leases, supplies, or has inherited access to a surprising amount of
Hayama.

This may include:

- parking lots
- storehouses
- alleys
- delivery corridors
- old sheds
- shrine-adjacent parcels
- festival storage rooms
- construction staging areas
- private roads
- keys to gates no one remembers assigning
- land held through old family companies or informal agreements

Daimon's real power is not force. It is access.

### Relationship To The Suzukis

Daimon is Suzuki-linked but not reducible to the Suzukis.

The Suzuki family may currently operate Daimon, control its land network, or use
it as their most visible public face. However, Daimon's access arrangements are
older than any one living Suzuki, and even the Suzukis may not know every door
Daimon can open.

This keeps Daimon grounded while preserving its hidden-boss quality.

### Story Function

Daimon is useful when the story needs:

- a way into places the party should not technically be able to access
- a neutral-seeming broker between factions
- a grounded local business with supernatural implications
- information from deliveries, purchases, favors, and old land arrangements
- a reminder that power in Hayama is often logistical rather than spectacular

Daimon can get the party:

- into a construction site
- onto private roads
- behind a shrine
- into storage sheds
- near old barrier-adjacent locations
- under municipal radar
- close to meetings between people who think they are being discreet

### Faction Relationships

- Civic Hayama: supplies events, knows permits, owns awkward parcels, and notices
  who buys apology gifts
- Anchored: may have old access agreements, storage arrangements, or ritual supply
  chains
- Drifted: offers quiet favors, meeting space, and access for people who know how
  to ask
- Neutral locals: trusted fixture, gossip hub, emergency supplier
- Tsunodas: necessary but suspicious; favors between them may go back generations
- Suzukis: current operators, heirs, or managers of Daimon's visible face
- PCs: potential fixer, quest giver, access broker, and complication

### Symbolism

Daimon symbolizes hidden ownership, social leverage, and the invisible
infrastructure of a town.

It asks: who really has access to a place, and what does access cost?

## How They Enter Act I

Use these groups quietly at first.

- A Daimon delivery van appears behind a locked gate near a reaping site.
- Someone says, "Ask Daimon. They've got a key."
- A Suzuki clerk or driver knows which officials visited the shoreline project.
- A Tsunoda recognizes a tide effect as bad inheritance rather than ordinary
  haunting.
- A Tsunoda or Suzuki notices Marin before Marin understands why.
- Daimon has a receipt, delivery route, or property record that contradicts a
  municipal account.
- The Tsunodas and Suzukis disagree publicly about whether the current crisis is
  a chance to restore something or merely survive something.

## Use In The Campaign Structure

### Act I: Misdiagnosis

The families complicate blame.

The party may reasonably suspect:

- the Anchored are hiding evidence
- civic officials covered up project damage
- the Tsunodas are stirring up Drifted anger
- the Suzukis are manipulating access
- Daimon knows more than a liquor store should

All may be partly true.

### Act II: System Reveal

The families provide counter-histories.

- The Anchored explain the Barrier as necessary containment.
- The Tsunodas explain the Barrier as suppression, theft, or spiritual injury.
- The Suzukis explain the Barrier as infrastructure everyone learned to survive
  around.
- Daimon explains nothing cleanly, but its old access records prove that hidden
  arrangements shaped the town.

### Act III: Compounding Failure

Their powers become useful but costly.

- Tsunoda rites can preserve memory through a reaping but attract divine
  attention.
- Suzuki access can open closed routes but binds the party into obligations.
- Daimon can get the party almost anywhere, but every door opened enters someone
  into a ledger.

### Act IV: Resolution

Their positions should sharpen the campaign question.

- Should damaged inheritance be restored, stabilized, or allowed to become
  something new?
- Should old arrangements be honored because they preserved the town, or broken
  because they hid the truth?
- Can Hayama survive without secret access networks, suppressed divinity, and
  inherited compromise?

## NPC Roles To Create Later

Do not create all of these immediately. Promote only the ones the campaign needs.

### Tsunoda Roles

- elder memory-keeper
- younger heir who wants action
- ritualist who can preserve names during reaping
- family skeptic who doubts the old grievance
- Marin-facing interpreter of displaced divinity

### Suzuki / Daimon Roles

- Daimon proprietor
- delivery driver / keyholder
- land records operator
- family heir who treats favors like currency
- friendly clerk who knows too much
- fixer who can arrange access to almost anywhere

## Table Guidance

When using these groups, keep their power grounded.

They should not replace the Anchored, the Drifted, or civic authority. They should
make the social field messier.

The best version is one where every faction is right about something and wrong
about what it means.
