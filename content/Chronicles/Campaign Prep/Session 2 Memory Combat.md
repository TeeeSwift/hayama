# Session 2 Memory Combat

## High-Level Overview

This encounter is the party's first real fight, but it should not teach them
that the Reaping can be fought.

The Reaping should remain vast, terrifying, and insurmountable in Act 1: less a
villain than an oceanic condition pressing against the town. The PCs can notice
its effects, survive near it, and preserve small pieces of truth from being
washed away, but they cannot meaningfully strike back at it yet.

This fight is instead against the Mayor's office-defense system.

The party is trying to obtain or preserve the old woman's Town Hall complaint.
When they intrude too far into protected civic records, the office's enchantments
activate. The ticketing machine becomes the center of the encounter, printing
impossible service tickets tied to different municipal departments. Each ticket
causes a small bureaucratic magical effect until the PCs deal with it.

The tone should be strange, active, and a little funny before it becomes ominous.
This is the first fight, so keep the danger readable and survivable. The horror
comes from realizing that the town has procedures for making people disappear,
not from facing the Reaping directly.

Core premise:

> The complaint is the goal. The ticketing machine is the engine. The Mayor's
> defenses are the enemy. The Reaping is only the distant pressure that makes
> the whole thing feel wrong.

## Scene Shape

Use this when the party enters, searches, or magically probes a protected office
space connected to the complaint.

The office initially looks ordinary:

- a reception counter
- fluorescent lights
- numbered service windows
- old posters about permits, records, and public works
- a ticketing machine near the entrance
- locked drawers or cabinets containing the complaint

Then the machine chirps.

It prints a ticket with one of the PCs' numbers on it.

The overhead display changes from a normal queue to something impossible:

> NOW SERVING: INTRUSION

The office-defense enchantments activate.

## Encounter Objective

The PCs are not trying to win a duel. They are trying to get the complaint and
leave with usable information.

Suggested objective:

> Recover the complaint, or at least one durable clue from it, before the office
> files it away, redacts it, or locks the party out.

The encounter can end when:

- the PCs secure the complaint
- the PCs extract a key clue and retreat
- the ticketing machine is disabled
- the office-defense system overloads or resets

Failure should cost clarity, time, or evidence quality. It should not end the
investigation.

## The Ticketing Machine

The ticketing machine is the central hazard.

At the start of each round, or whenever pacing needs a jolt, the machine prints
a new ticket. Each ticket belongs to a department and creates a matching magical
effect in the office.

Players can interact with the machine in many ways:

- pull a ticket before it finishes printing
- jam the slot
- press buttons for a different department
- feed it false information
- tear up active tickets
- use a ticket to access a matching office drawer or service window
- attack, unplug, or magically disrupt the machine

The machine should feel like a puzzle-object inside a fight, not just a monster
with hit points.

## Department Tickets

Use only a few of these in the actual encounter. Pick the ones that fit the
moment, or roll if the table wants chaos.

### Records Department

The system tries to control what can be known.

- A word on the complaint redacts itself.
- A filing cabinet tries to swallow the complaint.
- A PC briefly forgets why they came here.
- A copied note or photo blurs until the ticket is destroyed.

### Permits Department

The room decides who is authorized to stand where.

- Floor tiles become restricted zones.
- A door refuses to open without the correct ticket.
- A PC's weapon, spell, or movement is briefly "unpermitted."
- Desks slide to block unauthorized paths.

### Public Works

The office rearranges itself like a hostile municipal project.

- Filing cabinets become barriers.
- Wet floor signs appear and make areas slick.
- Ceiling panels drop dust, sparks, or old seawall grit.
- A strip of floor is marked for demolition or repair.

### Sanitation Department

The system tries to dispose of evidence.

- A trash bin or shredder animates.
- Loose papers slide toward disposal.
- Ink begins to drain from a document.
- The room smells of bleach, salt, and wet paper.

### Finance Department

The system imposes penalties.

- A PC receives a magical fine for obstruction.
- Refusing payment causes minor psychic pressure or disadvantage.
- Coins, keys, or metal objects pull toward a cash drawer.
- A "late fee" increases the effect if the ticket survives another round.

### Human Resources

The system assigns false identities and roles.

- Name badges appear with wrong names.
- A PC is labeled STAFF, VISITOR, WITNESS, or LIABILITY.
- The office only targets creatures it has successfully "identified."
- A PC is compelled to return to an assigned station.

### Mayor's Office

Use this sparingly, as an escalation.

- The room goes quiet.
- The Mayor's seal appears on a door, ticket, or document.
- Other active tickets resolve in the system's favor.
- The complaint moves one step closer to being sealed away.
- A polite recorded voice says: "This matter has already been resolved."

## Light Enemies

Keep the first fight simple. The ticketing machine is the memorable part.

Possible defenders:

- animated clipboards
- stamping hands
- paper constructs
- rolling office chairs
- a receptionist ward made of forms, badges, and phone cord
- filing cabinets that shove, pin, or separate PCs

These defenders should mostly obstruct, restrain, shove, confiscate, and redact.
They do not need to hit hard.

## PC Awakening Beats

This encounter can be where the PCs first access spells or supernatural actions.
Do not frame it as leveling up. Frame it as the office becoming thin enough that
their existing entanglements can act.

### Alex Gaga

Alex reads the room as rhythm: printer chimes, fluorescent hum, ticket numbers,
cabinet movement.

Possible expression:

- disrupting the machine's timing
- stabilizing a document by matching the office's pulse
- turning a bureaucratic command back on itself

### Vincent Uminashi

Vincent feels inheritance, civic authority, and the danger of being the person
who still remembers.

Possible expression:

- speaking with inherited authority
- recognizing which ticket is false
- standing in exactly the right place to interrupt the system

### Ryunnu

Ryunnu receives an impossible administrative prompt through the Mayor's mistaken
patron interface.

Possible prompt:

> UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS RESPONSE ACTIVE. TEMPORARY OVERRIDE AVAILABLE. ACCEPT? Y/N

Possible expression:

- denying a ticket's effect
- selecting an option the system did not mean to offer
- briefly using the Mayor's own defense protocol against itself

The Mayor should react with alarm, not exposition.

Possible line:

> That was not intended.

### Aizawa Marin

Marin senses the deep-sea wrongness behind the office magic, but the fight itself
is still municipal and constructed.

Possible expression:

- sensing which department ticket will print next
- redirecting pressure, cold, or water
- recognizing that the defense system is afraid of something larger than itself

## Reaping Boundary

Do not let the encounter imply that the party has beaten the Reaping.

If the Reaping appears at all, it should be indirect:

- a ticket prints with no number
- a name vanishes from a form before anyone can read it
- the office-defense system stutters or panics
- the Mayor's enchantment tries to seal the complaint faster
- the machine prints one final impossible department

Possible final ticket:

> DEPARTMENT: REAPING  
> NOW SERVING: NO ONE

Then the ticketing machine goes dead.

This should feel like a glimpse of something much larger, not a boss reveal.

## Ending The Encounter

When the office resets, everything should look almost normal again.

The floor is dry. The fluorescent lights hum. The ticketing machine is inert or
quietly smoking. The complaint is either in the party's hands, partially
redacted, or remembered through whatever clues they managed to preserve.

Useful rewards:

- the complaint itself
- the complaint number
- a clerk's initials
- a stamp leading to the Mayor's Office
- a department code that should not exist
- a phrase like "resolved administratively"
- proof that the office has active enchantments

The players should leave understanding one important thing:

> The town's bureaucracy is not just hiding the truth. It has been enchanted to
> defend the lie.
