# Hayama Locals and Rumors

## Purpose

Use this as a table-facing layer for making Hayama feel inhabited before the
larger cosmology is understood.

These NPCs, rumors, and recurring faces are deliberately modular. Promote only
the pieces the players engage with. Until promoted, treat them as useful social
texture rather than fixed major canon.

## Tone Targets

Hayama locals should feel:

- warm but observant
- accustomed to the sea as a personality without necessarily being mystical
- practiced at rationalizing strange events
- proud of the town, but divided over development
- more aware of absences and pattern breaks than they first admit

The social texture should reinforce Act I: Misdiagnosis. People have partial
truths, strong opinions, and plausible wrong explanations.

## Recurring Local Faces

### Kitamura Haru — Beachside Cafe Owner

- Place: small cafe near the dive site
- Function: cozy regroup point, rumor hub, food, witness traffic
- First impression: warm, gossipy, practical
- Knows: who has been acting strange, which tourists are missing, which officials
  came through recently
- Avoids: naming customers who no longer appear in other people's memories
- Useful scene: gives the party towels, tea, and too many opinions after a tide
  incident
- Voice: "Eat first. Panic after. Panic on an empty stomach makes people stupid."

### Fujino Masato — Local Fisherman

- Place: harbor, bait shop, or dockside smoking area
- Function: sea-lore, project criticism, grounded witness
- First impression: suspicious, observant, stubborn
- Knows: currents, old fishing routes, rumors from boat crews
- Avoids: admitting he is frightened by what his nets have pulled up
- Useful scene: shows shrine rope and municipal survey flags tangled together
- Voice: "They keep drawing lines on maps. The sea keeps taking them personally."

### Oda Fumi — Retired Ama Diver

- Place: shore wall, fish market, shrine steps, or cafe corner
- Function: old coastal memory and unsettling folk wisdom
- First impression: dry humor, sharp eyes, unimpressed by officials
- Knows: vanished shore names, old reef patterns, taboo phrases
- Avoids: direct accusations unless someone proves they can listen
- Useful scene: recognizes that a newly appeared reef is an older coastline
  returning
- Voice: "Young people call it erosion when the sea remembers where it slept."

### Moriyama Keisuke — Dive Instructor

- Place: diving school, beach, equipment shed
- Function: session 1 anchor, civilian safety, credible sea witness
- First impression: calm, sun-weathered, professionally cheerful
- Knows: local water conditions, which anomalies are genuinely impossible
- Avoids: causing panic among students or tourists
- Useful scene: quietly asks Marin whether she saw the reef too
- Voice: "The sea gets moods. This wasn't a mood."

### Tanabe Riko — First-Time Diver

- Place: diving class, cafe, guesthouse
- Function: sympathetic endangered civilian or witness
- First impression: nervous, talkative, eager to seem brave
- Knows: she heard someone call her name underwater before entering the sea
- Avoids: being treated like she panicked for no reason
- Useful scene: remembers a missing voice no one else heard
- Voice: "I thought it was one of you joking. But it sounded like my grandmother."

### Arai Chika — Middle School Student

- Place: seawall, convenience store, beach path, bus stop
- Function: child witness, rumor carrier, impossible drawing source
- First impression: bold, curious, too online, not easily frightened
- Knows: which adults lie badly, which places feel wrong after school
- Avoids: being sent home before the interesting part
- Useful scene: shows a map of Hayama with several streets underwater
- Voice: "Adults only call things impossible when they don't want to do paperwork."

### Hayashi — Municipal Coastal Works Liaison

- Place: inspection site, town office, public meeting
- Function: official explanations, project paperwork, narrative control
- First impression: efficient, apologetic, overprepared
- Knows: the shoreline project interacted with undocumented coastal stonework
- Avoids: admitting the wall may be causing something stranger than ordinary
  structural stress
- Useful scene: reframes a supernatural incident as a safety inspection issue
- Voice: "Let's not turn an engineering issue into a ghost story before we've
  checked the paperwork."

### Nishimura Saki — Junior Records Clerk

- Place: municipal archive, back office, convenience store after work
- Function: paperwork access, altered records, Ryunnu system weirdness
- First impression: anxious, sharp, too aware of procedural mistakes
- Knows: records have changed after being filed
- Avoids: becoming the scapegoat for impossible archival errors
- Useful scene: shows the party a permit copy that no longer matches the archive
- Voice: "I know what I filed. I also know that's not what the archive says now."

### Hasegawa Toru — Site Foreman

- Place: construction office, work truck, damaged shoreline site
- Function: practical witness to the strange behavior around the anchor point
- First impression: tired, blunt, protective of his crew
- Knows: coastal stonework should not make the tide go quiet
- Avoids: naming the worker who quit after hearing waves underground
- Useful scene: describes the impact before the site was covered again
- Voice: "Concrete cracks. Rebar sings. Ground settles. But waves do not just
  lose the beat."

### Suzume — Shrine Helper With Missing Context

- Place: coastal shrine, offering path, small festival stall
- Function: ritual texture, partial warnings, soft supernatural sensitivity
- First impression: gentle, formal, easily overwhelmed
- Knows: small rites, shell offerings, and which offerings are being returned
- Avoids: explaining that the previous caretaker may have vanished from memory
- Useful scene: asks the party not to open a returned shell offering
- Voice: "We leave shells for safe return. Lately, something has been returning
  them unopened."

### Iwase Ren — Drifted Agitator

- Place: public meeting, protest flyer table, back of the cafe
- Function: plausible anti-Anchored interpretation and social friction
- First impression: intense, persuasive, wounded
- Knows: something is being hidden and people are disappearing around the edges
- Avoids: admitting their explanation is incomplete
- Useful scene: accuses officials and old powers of turning maintenance into
  erasure
- Voice: "Every time something vanishes, someone powerful calls it maintenance."

### Shindo Natsume — Anchored Observer

- Place: drainage channels, old markers, shoreline edges
- Function: suspicious watcher who is actually monitoring barrier failure
- First impression: polite, evasive, physically still
- Knows: the diving disturbance is a symptom, not the cause
- Avoids: explaining the Barrier before trust or necessity demands it
- Useful scene: removes a barnacled fragment before officials notice it
- Voice: "Do not touch what the tide leaves behind unless you are prepared to be
  noticed."

## Local Opinion Axes

Use these to make conversations feel varied.

### Development

- pro-project: Hayama needs protection, jobs, tourism, and modern infrastructure
- anti-project: outsiders and officials are cutting into things they do not
  understand
- resigned: the project is just the newest version of people arguing with the
  shoreline

### The Sea

- practical: dangerous, beautiful, predictable if respected
- devotional: alive, listening, owed courtesy
- skeptical: weather and currents do not need stories
- frightened: something has changed and everyone is pretending otherwise

### The Mayor / Civic Authority

- trusting: someone has to keep the town functioning
- cynical: every safety notice is also a liability shield
- dependent: permits, grants, and tourism keep families afloat
- suspicious: officials know which records to lose

### The Old Families / Anchored

- respectful: old families remember things the town needs
- resentful: old families always know more than they say
- dismissive: prestige and superstition wearing the same clothes
- afraid: when they gather, something has already gone wrong

## Scene Starters

### Cafe After The Dive

Haru gives the party towels and hot drinks. A regular's usual seat is wet, but
Haru cannot remember who usually sits there.

Use for:

- decompression after the first incident
- rumors
- a local noticing Vincent
- a municipal official trying to control the story

### Records Office Mismatch

Saki shows two versions of the shoreline permit. One includes Alex's approval and
a routine geological note. The other has a blank line where an older structure
should be described.

Use for:

- Alex implication
- Ryunnu's erroneous authorization
- connection to the stabilization site

### Dockside Net

Masato or another fisher has hauled up rope, survey flags, and a shell that hums
near Marin.

Use for:

- linking mundane project work to sacred/Barrier material
- making the sea feel like it is returning evidence
- introducing Drifted or Anchored interest

### Shrine Offering Returned

Suzume asks for help because offerings for safe return have come back sealed,
wet, and heavier than before.

Use for:

- ritual texture
- soft dread
- a non-combat clue that the usual channels are failing

### Public Explanation

Hayashi or another official explains the incident as a safety matter. Iwase
Ren interrupts, accusing officials and old families of hiding disappearances.

Use for:

- social pressure
- public misdiagnosis
- giving the party several plausible wrong explanations

## Memory Distortion Prompts

Use sparingly. These work best when attached to something specific.

- A local skips a name in a list, then insists there was no blank.
- A photo has one extra wet person in the background.
- Someone's cup remains on a cafe table after everyone forgets they ordered.
- A child's drawing includes a person the adults cannot identify.
- A permit signature is legible only while wet.
- A street sign briefly uses an older name.
- A regular customer is remembered only by their usual order.
- A voicemail contains wave sounds under ordinary speech.

## Table Use Guidance

When uncertain, choose one cozy detail and one wrong detail.

Examples:

- cozy: hot canned coffee from a vending machine
- wrong: the can is beaded with seawater before opening

- cozy: an auntie complains about tourists blocking the road
- wrong: she cannot remember which road reaches the beach now

- cozy: kids chase each other with festival lanterns
- wrong: one lantern burns underwater-blue and nobody comments

The town should feel lovable first. The horror lands harder if Hayama feels worth
protecting.
