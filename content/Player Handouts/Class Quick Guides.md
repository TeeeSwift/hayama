# Class Quick Guides

This handout is for reading your character sheet during play. Your sheet is the
source of truth: if this guide and your sheet disagree, use your sheet and ask
the DM after the scene.

For full spell menus, see [[Player Handouts/Level 1 Spell Lists|Level 1 Spell Lists]].

These notes assume the 2024 core class rules for bard, druid, ranger, and
warlock. The Fathomless patron is from _Tasha's Cauldron of Everything_, so its
exact level timing may depend on whether your sheet uses 2014 or 2024 warlock
rules.

## Your Most Important Numbers

| Number | What It Means |
| --- | --- |
| AC | How hard you are to hit |
| HP | How much damage you can take |
| Speed | How far you move on your turn |
| Spell Save DC | The number enemies roll against when your spell forces a save |
| Spell Attack Bonus | The bonus you add when your spell makes an attack roll |
| Concentration Save | Usually a Constitution save to keep a spell going after damage |
| Passive Perception | What you might notice without actively searching |

Spell formulas, already calculated on most sheets:

- `Spell attack bonus = proficiency bonus + spellcasting ability modifier`
- `Spell save DC = 8 + proficiency bonus + spellcasting ability modifier`

Spellcasting abilities:

| Class | Spellcasting Ability |
| --- | --- |
| Warlock | Charisma |
| Ranger | Wisdom |
| Druid | Wisdom |
| Bard | Charisma |

## What You Can Do On A Turn

Most turns are built from:

- 1 Action
- 1 Bonus Action, if you have something that uses it
- Movement up to your Speed
- 1 Reaction, usually outside your turn when something triggers it

If two options both say "Bonus Action," you usually pick one.

## How Spells Work

Cantrips are your at-will spells. You can cast them without spending spell
slots.

Level 1+ spells usually spend spell slots. A spell slot is not a specific spell;
it is the fuel used to cast a spell you have prepared or available.

Each spell tells you how it works:

| Spell Entry | What To Check |
| --- | --- |
| Casting Time | Action, Bonus Action, Reaction, or longer |
| Range | How far away the target or effect can be |
| Components | What the spell requires: verbal, somatic, material |
| Duration | Instant, timed, or Concentration |
| Attack/Save | Whether you roll, the target rolls, or neither |

Three common spell patterns:

- Spell attack: you roll `d20 + spell attack bonus`.
- Saving throw spell: the target rolls against your Spell Save DC.
- No-roll spell: the effect happens if the spell's conditions are met.

## Concentration

Some spells say Concentration. You can concentrate on only one spell at a time.
If you cast a second Concentration spell, the first one ends.

If you take damage while concentrating, you may need a Constitution saving throw
to keep the spell:

`DC 10 or half the damage, whichever is higher`

Before casting a Concentration spell, ask:

> Is this the spell I want to hold onto right now?

## Warlock: Fathomless Patron

### What You Are Good At

You are a short-rest spellcaster with strange, focused magic. You are usually
good at:

- consistent ranged magical attacks
- Charisma skills like Deception, Intimidation, and Persuasion
- eerie ocean-themed control
- pushing, slowing, frightening, or isolating enemies
- solving weird problems with a small number of powerful spells

### Your Core Rhythm

Warlocks have fewer spell slots than most casters, but those slots come back on
a Short Rest. Your slots are precious, but you are not helpless without them:
your cantrips and invocations matter a lot.

Your default combat pattern is often:

1. Use a strong cantrip or weapon attack.
2. Spend a spell slot when the scene is important.
3. Use your patron feature or Bonus Action option when it changes the fight.
4. Ask for a Short Rest after a major scene if you spent your slots.

### Fathomless Things To Watch

If your sheet has the Fathomless patron features, look for:

| Feature Type | How To Think About It |
| --- | --- |
| Spectral tentacle | Bonus Action pressure, cold damage, and slowing |
| Ocean adaptation | Swimming and underwater scenes are your home turf |
| Expanded spells | Water, storm, cold, and battlefield control options |
| Defensive tentacle features | Later levels may let your tentacle protect allies |

Important: several warlock options compete for your Bonus Action. If you have a
tentacle, Hex, or another Bonus Action option, choose the one that matters most
this turn.

### Spell Picks To Understand

If these are on your sheet, here is when to think about using them:

| Spell | Use It When... |
| --- | --- |
| Eldritch Blast | You need a reliable ranged attack |
| Hex | You expect to keep hitting one target for several rounds |
| Armor of Agathys | You expect to be hit and want defense plus punishment |
| Hellish Rebuke | You want to punish someone who just damaged you |
| Thunderwave | You are surrounded or need to shove enemies away |
| Create or Destroy Water | Water itself is the problem or solution |
| Silence | Sound, speech, casting, or stealth matters |
| Gust of Wind | Positioning, movement, fog, gas, or pressure matters |

### Ask Yourself

- Am I saving my slot for a major moment, or is this the major moment?
- Do I need damage, control, escape, or information?
- Is my Bonus Action already spoken for?
- Would a Short Rest afterward make sense?

## Ranger

### What You Are Good At

You are a practical explorer and martial spellcaster. You are usually good at:

- weapon attacks from range or melee
- tracking, scouting, and noticing danger
- moving through wilderness or rough terrain
- focusing damage on one important target
- mixing weapons, movement, and nature magic

### Your Core Rhythm

Rangers often solve problems before they become fights. In combat, you usually
want to attack steadily while using a small number of spells to mark, trap,
heal, reveal, or reposition.

Your default combat pattern is often:

1. Choose the most important target.
2. Attack with your best weapon.
3. Use Hunter's Mark or another spell if it will matter for more than one turn.
4. Move to a position where you can keep attacking without being pinned down.

### Ranger Magic

Ranger spells use Wisdom. In 2024 rules, rangers start spellcasting at level 1.
Older sheets may start ranger spells at level 2.

Ranger spells are often:

- tracking tools
- weapon enhancers
- terrain control
- emergency healing
- animal and nature communication

Vincent Uminashi currently has Hunter's Mark always prepared, plus Animal
Friendship and Longstrider prepared as his level 1 ranger spells. He has
Shortsword and Handaxe as his weapon masteries.

### Spell Picks To Understand

If these are on your sheet, here is when to think about using them:

| Spell | Use It When... |
| --- | --- |
| Hunter's Mark | You will attack the same target repeatedly |
| Cure Wounds | Someone needs healing and you can reach them |
| Goodberry | You want reliable out-of-combat healing or survival food |
| Ensnaring Strike | You want a weapon hit to restrain or pin a target |
| Hail of Thorns | You want a weapon hit to splash damage nearby targets |
| Fog Cloud | You need cover, escape, confusion, or concealment |
| Detect Magic | Something supernatural may be present |
| Speak with Animals | An animal might know what happened |
| Longstrider | Movement will matter for a while |

### Watch Your Concentration

Many ranger spells use Concentration. Hunter's Mark is good, but it can block
other Concentration spells. If you need Fog Cloud, Detect Magic, or Ensnaring
Strike, you may need to let Hunter's Mark go.

### Ask Yourself

- Is this a fight where Hunter's Mark will pay off?
- Can I learn something before attacking?
- Is my best contribution damage, tracking, healing, or control?
- Am I spending magic to solve a real problem, or can a skill check do it?

## Druid

### What You Are Good At

You are a full spellcaster tied to nature, bodies, weather, animals, and the
land. You are usually good at:

- healing and recovery
- controlling the battlefield
- noticing natural or supernatural imbalance
- speaking with or influencing animals and plants
- changing the environment instead of just damaging enemies
- Wild Shape utility once your sheet gives it to you

### Your Core Rhythm

Druids prepare spells and regain spell slots on a Long Rest. You can often
change your prepared spells after resting, so you are flexible from day to day.

Your default combat pattern is often:

1. Start with a control spell if the fight needs shaping.
2. Use healing when someone is in danger, not just lightly scratched.
3. Use cantrips when the situation is stable.
4. Keep Concentration safe if your current spell is carrying the scene.

### Druid Magic

Druid spells use Wisdom. Your magic is especially good when the scene involves:

- water, weather, plants, animals, stone, fire, or disease
- hidden natural signs
- protecting allies
- making enemies waste movement or actions
- turning the environment into an advantage

Marin currently knows Druidcraft and Shillelagh as cantrips. Her level 1 druid
spells can be prepared after a Long Rest, so they do not need to be fixed yet.
If using 2024 rules, Speak with Animals is always prepared from Druidic.

### Spell Picks To Understand

If these are on your sheet, here is when to think about using them:

| Spell | Use It When... |
| --- | --- |
| Guidance | Someone is about to make an important ability check |
| Cure Wounds | Someone needs healing and you can reach them |
| Healing Word | Someone needs healing from a distance or while you use your Action elsewhere |
| Entangle | You want to slow, restrain, or divide enemies |
| Faerie Fire | You want allies to hit easier and reveal hidden targets |
| Fog Cloud | You need cover, confusion, escape, or concealment |
| Thunderwave | You are surrounded or need to force space |
| Speak with Animals | An animal could be a witness or guide |
| Detect Magic | The scene may contain active magic |
| Goodberry | You want reliable out-of-combat healing or survival food |

### Wild Shape

If Wild Shape is on your sheet, it can be used for:

- scouting
- sneaking
- swimming, climbing, or fitting somewhere unusual
- surviving danger
- interacting with animals or nature from a different angle

Do not worry about memorizing every beast form. Pick a few simple forms that fit
your character and ask the DM when a scene suggests one.

### Ask Yourself

- Would changing the battlefield help more than dealing damage?
- Is this a healing emergency or can it wait?
- Which Concentration spell matters most right now?
- Can nature give us information before we spend resources?

## Bard

### What You Are Good At

You are a social, magical, skillful support character. You are usually good at:

- talking, performing, persuading, and reading the room
- helping allies succeed at key rolls
- healing at clutch moments
- illusions, emotions, charm, fear, and sound
- knowing a little bit about many things
- turning a messy scene into an opportunity

### Your Core Rhythm

Bards use Charisma for spells. You are often strongest when you improve someone
else's moment or make an enemy lose theirs.

Your default combat pattern is often:

1. Give Bardic Inspiration before an ally's important moment.
2. Use a control or support spell when the scene needs a swing.
3. Use a cantrip or weapon when you are saving resources.
4. Keep a healing option ready for emergencies.

### Bardic Inspiration

Bardic Inspiration is one of your signature tools. In 2024-style rules, you use
a Bonus Action to give an ally an inspiration die. They can spend it later when
it might turn a failed d20 roll into a success.

Use it before:

- a dangerous save
- a clutch attack
- a vital social roll
- a stealth, investigation, or escape attempt

If your sheet uses 2014 rules, ask the DM exactly when the die is spent. The
spirit is the same: you make someone else's important roll better.

### Spell Picks To Understand

If these are on your sheet, here is when to think about using them:

| Spell | Use It When... |
| --- | --- |
| Vicious Mockery | You want light damage plus making an enemy less effective |
| Healing Word | Someone drops or needs help from a distance |
| Cure Wounds | Someone needs stronger touch-range healing |
| Dissonant Whispers | You want damage plus forced movement or panic |
| Charm Person | A social scene might turn with magical friendliness |
| Faerie Fire | You want allies to hit easier and reveal hidden targets |
| Sleep or Color Spray | You want to disable weaker enemies early |
| Disguise Self | Appearance can solve the problem |
| Silent Image or Minor Illusion | Deception, distraction, or misdirection helps |

### Watch Your Bonus Action

Bardic Inspiration and Healing Word both use your Bonus Action. On a tense turn,
you may need to choose between helping someone succeed and helping someone stay
up.

### Ask Yourself

- Who is about to make the most important roll?
- Can I solve this with words before spending a spell?
- Is this a control moment, a healing moment, or an inspiration moment?
- Would an illusion or performance change what people believe is happening?

## During Play, Ask The DM

- "Does this spell require Concentration?"
- "Is this an Action or Bonus Action?"
- "Do I roll a spell attack, or do they make a save?"
- "Can I use this skill because of how I described it?"
- "Would this be a good moment for my class feature?"
