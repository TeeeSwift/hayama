# Session 2 Prep

## Purpose

Session 2 should turn the first reaping into actionable pressure.

The players should leave knowing that the cafe event, the unsecured phone, the
seawall complaint, and [[Actors/Player Characters/Ryunnu|Ryunnu]]'s patron
connection all point toward the same civic-supernatural failure, without yet
understanding [[Lore/The Barrier|the Barrier]].

Act I is still Misdiagnosis. Let the players suspect infrastructure failure,
cover-up, strange civic records, and [[Actors/The Anchored|Anchored]]
interference before they know the cosmology.

## Session 2 Goals

- Let the PCs process the cafe reaping socially before they turn it into an
  investigation.
- Establish that the old woman was an innocent victim, not a secret-holder.
- Put the abandoned phone in the players' hands as the first concrete trace of
  someone the town is forgetting.
- Make memory erasure feel gradual for records and relationships, even though
  living memory failed quickly.
- Point the likely path toward the phone, then the seawall site, then the people
  involved in the project.
- Let the PCs begin awakening in specific, personal ways without explaining the
  [[Actors/The Anchored|Anchored]], [[Actors/The Drifted|Drifted]], or
  [[Lore/The Barrier|Barrier]] yet.
- Seed [[Actors/Barnacle|Barnacle]] and Daimon/Suzuki as ordinary maintenance and
  access figures before they become obvious faction leads.

## Likely Plot Path

The expected player flow:

1. Check the abandoned phone.
2. Use the call log or speed dial contacts to learn about a mundane
   [[Atlas/Town Hall|Town Hall]] complaint.
3. Visit the [[Atlas/Uminashi Seawall|Uminashi seawall]] site, especially the
   third span.
4. Follow [[Actors/Chizuru|Chizuru]]'s reply to identify the people and groups
   involved in the seawall project.
5. Discover that Daimon/Suzuki access, post-completion maintenance, and
   [[Actors/Barnacle|Barnacle]]'s suspicious containment work overlap around the
   same place.

## Immediate Social Aftermath

Do not rush straight into investigation. Let the PCs first try to verify reality.

The cafe becomes normal too fast:

- Staff remember a spill, a light flicker, a dropped cup, or a wet floor.
- Nobody remembers the old woman.
- The extra cup, wet chair leg, receipt/order oddity, and phone remain.
- [[Actors/Hayashi|Hayashi]] has already left before the reaping, so no official
  figure is present to absorb or explain the event.

The four PCs do not need to become a party immediately. Their first shared
identity is simply:

> We are the people who cannot go home yet.

Possible social progression:

1. Everyone checks the room and realizes other customers are smoothing the event
   into something ordinary.
2. [[Actors/Player Characters/Aizawa Marin|Marin]] asks her friend whether she
   saw or felt anything and receives a normal, concerned answer.
3. [[Actors/Player Characters/Ryunnu|Ryunnu]] realizes the voice in his head was
   only heard by him, but the old woman was seen by all four PCs.
4. [[Actors/Player Characters/Alex Gaga|Alex]] and
   [[Actors/Player Characters/Vincent Uminashi|Vincent]] are shaken by the
   vanished woman and the way the room immediately moves on.
5. Someone notices the abandoned phone and the group has to decide whether to
   protect it.

The temporary agreement can be modest:

> We check the phone, then the site, then the people involved. After that, we
> decide what this is.

## Things To Mention To Players

Use these as table-facing reminders or prompts.

For [[Actors/Player Characters/Aizawa Marin|Marin]]:

> The rift was terrifying, but whatever waited beyond it felt familiar. Not safe.
> Not friendly. Familiar, like hearing your name spoken from very far below.

This connects the cafe event to the dive without requiring Marin to care about
the seawall yet. Her curiosity comes from the deep-sea familiarity and from her
father's earlier warning about the [[Atlas/Shinnase|Shinnase]] area.

For [[Actors/Player Characters/Ryunnu|Ryunnu]]:

> You saw the woman vanish. Everyone else is already forgetting. Then a voice
> that was not yours asked whether the room lost someone.

Ryunnu does not need to stay with the others. It is acceptable if he goes home.
If he leaves, hook him back later through the Mayor's misaddressed connection,
Marin contacting him about the punctured regulator, or the town again treating
him as the person to call when something breaks.

For [[Actors/Player Characters/Alex Gaga|Alex]]:

> You do not have a clean professional category for what happened, but the timing
> with the shoreline meeting is hard to ignore.

For [[Actors/Player Characters/Vincent Uminashi|Vincent]]:

> [[Actors/Chizuru|Chizuru]]'s reply about the seawall gives you a way to turn
> shock into concrete names, records, and access.

## Opening: Ryunnu And The Mayor

Start with [[Actors/Player Characters/Ryunnu|Ryunnu]] in the reaping aftermath.
He witnessed the same vanishing as the others, but he also received the Mayor
through the unintended patron interface.

Keep the interaction fragmentary and bureaucratic rather than grand.

Possible sensations:

- A second pulse behind Ryunnu's own heartbeat.
- A polite but panicked administrative presence checking whether a channel is
  live.
- The taste of brine and photocopier toner.
- A form number, municipal seal, or access request flashing behind his eyes.
- A voice that sounds embarrassed to be using the wrong extension.

Possible Mayor lines:

- "This is not the correct channel."
- "You were not supposed to be connected to that."
- "Please remain calm. I appreciate that this is an unreasonable request."
- "Did the room lose someone?"
- "Do not touch anything that remembers better than people do."

Do not let the Mayor explain himself. He should want information, realize the
connection is unstable, and cut out before Ryunnu can get clean answers.

## Immediate Cafe Evidence

The cafe should become ordinary again too quickly.

Surviving clues:

- An extra coffee cup.
- A wet chair leg.
- A receipt or order number that staff cannot explain.
- The old woman's phone.
- Marin remembering the pressure of the sea while nearby customers remember only
  a spilled drink or a lights flicker.

The old woman was an innocent [[Actors/The Drifted|Drifted]] resident. She did
not understand divinity, [[Lore/The Barrier|the Barrier]], [[Actors/Watatsumi]],
or why the sea took her. Do not make her a secret lore-holder. The horror is
that an ordinary person vanished and the world is slowly making that ordinary
absence official.

Use a soft erasure clock:

- Living memory failed immediately.
- Physical and bureaucratic traces remain, but begin degrading.
- Relational traces linger longest, especially when someone cares enough to
  insist she existed.

## The Grandma Phone

The phone has no security. It is funny and useful that the most important clue is
protected by absolutely nothing.

Contents:

- Zero text messages.
- Photos of flowers, dogs, friends, cafe visits, and other ordinary life.
- Many phone call logs.
- Three speed-dial contacts such as "Daughter", "Clinic", and
  "Nagisabashi Coffee".
- Huge font size and a loud ringtone.

Useful photo clues:

- A blurry selfie at [[Atlas/Nagisabashi Coffee]] with a third coffee cup visible.
- Harmless seaside walk photos where the [[Atlas/Uminashi Seawall|Uminashi
  seawall]] happens to appear in the background.
- Dog and flower photos that feel personal, not investigative.
- Family or friend photos that prove she had ordinary life and ordinary ties.

Useful call-log clues:

- Repeated calls to [[Atlas/Town Hall|Town Hall]].
- One recent call from a public-facing municipal number.
- Multiple calls to [[Atlas/Nagisabashi Coffee]], because she was a regular.
- A final outgoing call shortly before the cafe event.

The phone should not explain the supernatural. It should identify an ordinary
person who called ordinary channels about an ordinary concern.

The Town Hall complaint should be innocuous. Something like:

> Resident reports repeated skateboard activity along the north seawall path,
> third span. Concern that youths may roll into the road or collide with delivery
> vehicles. Requests signage, barrier tape, or patrol reminder. Notes pavement is
> often wet even on clear days.

The party may initially assume she knew something. She did not. Her complaint
only matters because it caused attention to land on the third span.

## Hayashi And Procedure

[[Actors/Hayashi|Hayashi]] responds with calm procedure.

He may:

- classify the cafe event as an electrical issue, plumbing issue, or panic event
  caused by localized flooding
- ask the PCs not to spread rumors
- confirm the exposed foundation and third-party structural audit
- refuse to identify the original complainant without proper process
- mention that records are at [[Atlas/Town Hall|Town Hall]]
- treat Alex as professionally relevant
- treat Vincent as politically useful
- treat Marin as a sincere but unreliable youth witness
- be unsettled by Ryunnu if Ryunnu mentions the Mayor connection

Hayashi is not a villain. He is trying to keep the town calm with the tools he
has.

## Chizuru's Seawall Reply

[[Actors/Chizuru|Chizuru]] can answer
[[Actors/Player Characters/Vincent Uminashi|Vincent]] with a tidy involvement
map rather than a smoking gun.

Useful contents:

- The wall passed final inspection two months ago.
- No open defects were recorded at handover.
- [[Actors/Hayashi|Hayashi]]'s office handled municipal coordination.
- A mundane civil engineering firm handled design review.
- Daimon-linked logistics handled local staging and delivery access.
- Suzuki-affiliated parcels or routes were used for temporary storage.
- Uminashi Holdings granted shoreline access consent, not construction approval.
- Chizuru is checking post-completion maintenance logs.

This lead should point toward who had access, who noticed the erosion after the
fact, and who is now trying to manage the aftermath. It should not imply the
third span was initially built wrong.

The strongest twist is that the audit can be concerning but technically pass:

- significant erosion and foundation exposure are real
- the erosion rate is strange for a wall this new
- the load-bearing structure remains within tolerance
- there is no obvious construction defect
- the recommended response is monitoring, localized reinforcement, drainage
  review, and a follow-up scan

This lets the official answer be "concerning, but manageable" while the deeper
truth remains that the wall is rhythmically catastrophic for the
[[Lore/The Barrier|Barrier]].

Longer-term truth: the buried anchor is a medallion or medallion-like divine
piece that can be reached through old access paths. Removing or redesigning the
wall is the crude, reliable fix because it restores conditions closest to the
old wave rhythm, but it is not the only conceivable fix.

Other possible strategies:

- stabilize the anchor in place with temporary patches, false anchors, resonance
  marks, redirected water paths, or containment work
- relocate the medallion through old access routes, then recalibrate the
  surrounding Barrier
- buy time with Barnacle-style field repairs while the Anchored decide which
  risk is survivable

Do not reveal this cleanly in Session 2. The player-facing mystery should be:

> Why are people fighting over access to something under a wall that is not
> technically failing?

## Cafe Maintenance Seed

If [[Actors/Player Characters/Ryunnu|Ryunnu]] leaves the cafe, let him pass a
maintenance or delivery figure who seems ordinary now and important later.

Best low-friction version:

- A Daimon-linked worker arrives for a mundane cafe task: back-room lock,
  refrigerator check, storage delivery, soda line, festival crate pickup, or
  service entrance issue.
- They have a key or access that seems normal in the moment.
- They may casually mistake Ryunnu for the electrician.

Optional extra seed:

- [[Actors/Barnacle|Barnacle]] is also nearby, crouched near an exterior drain or
  threshold, scraping salt or shell grit into a small bag.
- The Daimon worker explains this away with something like "specialist" or
  "maintenance."

Do not make the seed demand attention. It should only become meaningful when
the same access network appears around the seawall, Town Hall, or project
records.

## Barnacle Encounter

[[Actors/Barnacle|Barnacle]] should appear soon, ideally before the players feel
too settled.

Good locations:

- outside [[Atlas/Nagisabashi Coffee]], scraping residue from a drain
- near the [[Atlas/Uminashi Seawall|Uminashi seawall]], sealing something under a
  maintenance cover
- in a [[Atlas/Town Hall|Town Hall]] service corridor after hours

Barnacle should look like a maintenance worker doing something suspiciously
specific.

They might collect:

- shell-like residue from a junction box
- salt crystals from a cafe floor crack
- a fragment of fresh concrete with tiny barnacles on it
- a municipal work tag that should not exist yet

If confronted:

> If you saw this, then you are already too close.

Barnacle should not attack first unless the party tries to take evidence or
force answers. Their job is containment, not exposition.

## Town Hall Break-In Option

The party may decide to identify the person who made the specific infrastructure
complaint.

Useful targets:

- complaint intake logs
- coastal works records
- scanned inspection request
- coast guard scan request
- visitor log
- Hayashi's calendar
- public works contractor file

Possible discoveries:

- The complaint came from an old woman whose name no longer sticks in the
  archive.
- The complaint number exists, but the complainant field is blank or changes
  when unobserved.
- The scanned phone number matches the erased woman's call log.
- The complaint specifically mentioned skateboarders near the third span of the
  north wall.
- Someone copied the complaint to a restricted office connected to the Mayor.
- Post-completion maintenance logs show ordinary erosion concerns around the
  third span, not a clean initial construction defect.

Keep the break-in grounded: locked doors, cameras, keycards, clerks, old file
rooms, quiet fluorescent lights. Then let one impossible detail intrude.

## Seawall Evidence

The erosion is real and confirmed, but the site should not reduce to "magic
damage, no paper trail." The wall can look like a successful modern structure
with a serious but manageable post-completion maintenance problem.

The waves should not be visibly wrong to a normal person.

Instead, reveal the rhythm failure through specialized or supernatural
perception:

- Alex sees that the structural failure pattern does not match the visible wave
  exposure.
- Marin feels a skipped beat in the water rather than seeing abnormal waves.
- Ryunnu receives an impossible authorization prompt near a utility box or
  inspection panel.
- Vincent notices an old family boundary marker, undocumented access route, or
  Chizuru-shaped omission in the official story.
- Barnacle can read the stress in concrete or salt deposits by touch.

The public explanation remains plausible: foundation exposure, erosion,
groundwater pressure, or a maintenance issue after completion. Avoid making the
initial construction a simple defect.

## Wall-Related Issues To Pick From

Use these as realistic problems that can later be explained by the wall's
interaction with the [[Lore/The Barrier|Barrier]], [[Actors/Watatsumi|Watatsumi]],
or [[Actors/The Anchored|Anchored]] pressure campaign. Do not use all of them at
once.

Physical site issues:

- Pavement stays damp near the third span.
  - Mundane: drainage grading issue or groundwater seepage.
  - Magical: the sea is returning through the wrong path.
- Salt crust forms in repeating bands.
  - Mundane: spray pattern and evaporation.
  - Magical: failed wave rhythm leaving timing marks.
- Rust appears too quickly on railings or fasteners.
  - Mundane: marine exposure or cheap hardware.
  - Magical: metal aging in sync with reclaiming pulses.
- Fine sand accumulates uphill from the wall.
  - Mundane: wind, foot traffic, road dust.
  - Magical: the sea moving material back toward the anchor.
- Hairline cracks recur after patching.
  - Mundane: thermal expansion or settling.
  - Magical: repairs do not hold because the rhythm underneath is misaligned.
- Small voids form under paving stones.
  - Mundane: washout or poor compaction.
  - Magical: the Barrier losing attachment to the surface.
- Drain covers rattle at low tide.
  - Mundane: pressure changes or loose fittings.
  - Magical: the anchor pulsing through municipal drainage.
- Unusual barnacle growth appears on shaded concrete.
  - Mundane: larvae caught in splash zones.
  - Magical: Barnacle or the sea marking stressed points.
- Concrete sounds hollow when tapped.
  - Mundane: voiding behind the wall.
  - Magical: spiritual desynchronization beneath the physical structure.

Water behavior:

- Waves hit the wall with an odd double beat.
  - Mundane: reflected wave interference.
  - Magical: the Barrier timing signal stuttering.
- Water pools after clear weather.
  - Mundane: seepage or drainage backup.
  - Magical: the sea arriving without the tide.
- Foam gathers only near one joint.
  - Mundane: turbulence from wall geometry.
  - Magical: the anchor trying to speak through foam pattern.
- Tide marks appear too high.
  - Mundane: storm surge residue or inaccurate observation.
  - Magical: Watatsumi reclaiming height without full flood.
- Small eddies persist near the third span.
  - Mundane: wall geometry.
  - Magical: the sea circling the damaged rhythm point.
- Water seems colder near one access panel.
  - Mundane: shade or underground flow.
  - Magical: deep-sea presence bleeding through.
- Fish avoid the area.
  - Mundane: noise or construction disturbance.
  - Magical: animals sensing the rhythm failure.
- Dogs refuse to cross the path.
  - Mundane: smell, vibration, or owner anxiety.
  - Magical: animals perceiving the wrongness more cleanly than people.

Civic and maintenance issues:

- Inspection QR code scans to the wrong record.
  - Mundane: database error.
  - Magical: records rerouting around erased traces.
- Maintenance tags appear without matching work orders.
  - Mundane: contractor miscommunication.
  - Magical: Anchored field work.
- Work orders close themselves as "resolved."
  - Mundane: clerical error.
  - Magical: the system smoothing over contradictions.
- Contractor photos have missing timestamps.
  - Mundane: bad metadata.
  - Magical: time around the third span degrading.
- Different maps show slightly different access routes.
  - Mundane: old parcel data.
  - Magical: old sacred boundaries bleeding into modern records.
- Insurance asks for extra review.
  - Mundane: risk flag from the erosion scan.
  - Magical: mundane institutions reacting to supernatural pressure.
- The town receives many small complaints.
  - Mundane: resident anxiety after a public safety notice.
  - Magical: Anchored pressure campaign amplifying concern.
- Repair materials arrive before anyone admits ordering them.
  - Mundane: logistics mix-up.
  - Magical: Daimon/Suzuki access network moving ahead of official process.

Human-scale weirdness:

- Skateboarders like one stretch because it hums.
  - Mundane: smooth pavement resonance.
  - Magical: the wall vibrating with the broken rhythm.
- Locals report headaches near the path.
  - Mundane: wind, stress, reflected sound.
  - Magical: low-level Barrier pressure.
- People forget why they walked there.
  - Mundane: distraction and routine.
  - Magical: memory softening near the failure point.
- Workers avoid looking under the third span.
  - Mundane: fear of liability.
  - Magical: instinctive avoidance of the anchor wound.
- Someone hears a phone ringing near the wall.
  - Mundane: lost phone or tinnitus.
  - Magical: erased traces trying to persist.
- A pedestrian remembers seeing an old woman there, then loses the detail.
  - Mundane: ordinary uncertainty.
  - Magical: her relational trace fading.
- Kids dare each other to touch one wet patch.
  - Mundane: ordinary teen behavior.
  - Magical: the spot feels charged or alive.

Best first picks:

- The audit says significant erosion, but the wall is still within tolerance.
- Pavement near the third span is often wet even on clear days.
- Skateboarders like the third span because the pavement is smooth and hums.
- A maintenance tag exists with no matching work order.
- A Daimon delivery or access record appears around the same time.
- Marin feels the water rhythm skip there.
- Alex sees that the visible erosion is real, but not enough to explain the
  deeper wrongness.
- Barnacle has patched or marked something in a way that looks suspiciously
  competent.

## Anchored Pressure Campaign

GM-facing working theory: [[Actors/The Anchored|the Anchored]] need the buried
anchor stabilized quickly because the seawall disrupts the wave rhythm required
by the [[Lore/The Barrier|Barrier]]. Bringing the wall down or forcing a redesign
is the most reliable known fix, but they may also be testing stabilization in
place or relocation through old access paths.

They may have tried exploiting or accelerating erosion first, but the wall is too
competent to fail cleanly. To make it structurally indefensible, they would have
to cause damage so extreme it would look like sabotage or an impossible event.

Their current strategy may be to make the wall politically, legally,
financially, and socially untenable:

- make post-completion maintenance inconsistencies visible
- encourage resident complaints and public concern
- make access irregularities matter
- pressure old families and civic officials
- make keeping the wall more expensive, embarrassing, and risky than removing or
  redesigning it

This reframes suspicious activity:

- [[Actors/Barnacle|Barnacle]] may preserve evidence, remove evidence, or patch
  damage depending on which action best keeps the pressure campaign controlled.
- Daimon/Suzuki access may support ordinary logistics, quiet pressure, or both.
- [[Actors/Hayashi|Hayashi]] is stuck defending a project that is not technically
  failing but is becoming harder to defend.
- Different Anchored or adjacent factions may prefer different solutions: wall
  removal, redesign, in-place stabilization, or relocation.

Act I question:

> Who is trying to make a functional wall look indefensible, and what are they so
> afraid of under it?

## Combat: Tide-Claimed Manifestation

The cleanest session 2 combat can happen in one of three places:

- Nagisabashi's back room if the party stays near the cafe evidence.
- A Town Hall records room after the party opens the wrong file cabinet.
- A seawall service access point after Barnacle seals something too late.

Use a small manifestation of Watatsumi's reclaiming, not a mastermind monster.

Concept:

An animated knot of seawater, crab-shell residue, wet receipts, grit, and
memory-static forms around a surviving piece of evidence. It is trying to pull
remembered traces of the erased woman out of the world.

Combat goals:

- Protect the phone, receipt, complaint record, or call log.
- Stop the manifestation from destroying records.
- Keep civilians from seeing too much.
- Let Barnacle intervene in a suspiciously useful way if needed.

Tactics:

- It shorts lights and equipment.
- It pulls unsecured objects toward drains, vents, or floor cracks.
- It creates difficult terrain as water spreads.
- It targets whoever is holding evidence.
- It briefly speaks in phone-call fragments from the erased woman's logs.

Flavor attacks:

- Brine arc through exposed wiring.
- Saltwater hand from a filing cabinet.
- Crab-shell scrape across concrete.
- Pressure pulse that makes speech sound muffled underwater.

Victory clue:

When defeated, it leaves behind a tiny residue pattern that matches the third
span of the north wall or a complaint file number.

## Session End Options

End at whichever pressure point the players choose:

- They identify the complaint but the complainant field erases itself.
- Barnacle escapes with half the evidence.
- The Mayor accidentally opens the patron channel again and asks Ryunnu,
  "What did it take?"
- Chizuru answers Vincent's question with one careful omission too many.
- The recovered clue points directly to the third span of the north wall.

The next session question should be:

Who benefits from treating a supernatural failure as an infrastructure problem?
