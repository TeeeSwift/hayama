# Session 2 Memory Combat

## Purpose

This encounter gives the party a real combat-like scene in Session 2 without
turning the campaign into a beat-them-up.

The fight should not feel like the sea spawned a monster for the party to kill.
It should feel like the PCs successfully preserved a memory, and that act made
other half-erased memories desperate to be remembered too.

Core premise:

> The party stabilizes the old woman's complaint. In doing so, they create a
> temporary memory-anchor. The old woman's late husband, whose last living anchor
> was her memory of him, senses a chance to become real again. His related
> memories manifest and fight the PCs because they believe the party is keeping
> him from reunion.

This keeps the combat grounded in the reaping, the complaint, the old woman, and
the campaign's core image of memory being erased like waves smoothing a sandy
shore.

## Scene Trigger

Use this after the party obtains or views the printed Town Hall complaint.

The complaint begins to degrade physically:

- Ink softens at the letter edges.
- The complainant field fades in curved bands, like tide lines.
- The name does not vanish all at once. It erodes millimeter by millimeter.
- The remaining ink forms shoreline-like contours.
- The paper becomes faintly damp without any visible source of water.

The PCs may try to preserve it by copying the text, photographing it, speaking the
name aloud, touching the paper, casting a spell, invoking authority, or simply
insisting that the woman existed.

Let them succeed.

For one breath, the complaint holds.

The ink stops moving. The name remains. The room goes quiet.

Then the complaint number rings like a phone.

## Transition Into The Reclaimed Room

Do not transport the party to a separate dimension. Instead, let the same room be
seen through the reclaiming.

The fluorescent lights remain, but sound distant and underwater. Filing cabinets
stand ankle-deep in dark water. Cabinet labels blur into strips of salt. Every
flat surface has tide marks. The complaint is dry at the center of the room,
impossibly protected by the attention the PCs have given it.

An elderly man appears near the edge of the light.

He is not monstrous. He is incomplete.

He looks at the complaint as if he recognizes it before he can read it.

Possible lines:

- "Keiko?"
- "She knew me."
- "I was real."
- "I almost had it."
- "Please."

He is the old woman's late husband. He is not a villain and should not be run as
the primary enemy. He is the emotional center of the encounter.

## What The Husband Wants

The husband believes the complaint is a way back to the remembered world.

He is partly right. The complaint is a stabilized trace of Keiko, and Keiko was
his last living memory-anchor. When she was reaped, the last person who actively
remembered him vanished too.

He does not understand the cost.

If he reaches the complaint and fully attaches himself to it, he may briefly
become clear, coherent, and remembered. But the complaint will collapse under the
weight of carrying more than it can hold. Keiko's trace, the civic clue, and the
husband's return will all fail together.

The tragedy:

> The memories are not evil. They are trying to reunite two people who loved each
> other. They are wrong about what the complaint can do.

## What The PCs Fight

The PCs fight the husband's related memories.

These are tangible enough to hit, shove, restrain, banish, or damage. They are
not generic monsters. They are incomplete memories trying to complete a story.

Each memory has a simple emotional logic. It sees the husband, sees the
complaint, sees the PCs in between, and tries to remove the obstacle.

### The Dog

A beloved family dog, not a horror dog.

It remembers that its people belong together. It does not understand death,
reaping, civic evidence, or supernatural failure. It only understands separation.

Combat behavior:

- Rushes PCs who stand between the husband and the complaint.
- Knocks people prone.
- Drags loose objects or unattended evidence toward the husband.
- Barks with the sound of an old phone ringing underneath it.

When defeated:

The dog does not die. It remembers its name for one second, wags its tail, and
fades.

### The Younger Husband

A younger, idealized version of the husband.

He remembers vows, persistence, pride, and the promise not to leave her alone. He
is more certain than the real husband because he is less complete.

Combat behavior:

- Grapples or shoves PCs away from the path.
- Keeps standing back up after being dropped.
- Pulls the older husband toward the complaint.
- May repeat a wedding vow without understanding the words.

When defeated:

He remembers the wedding date or the weather that day, smiles with sudden
clarity, and fades.

### The Caretaker

A memory of Keiko caring for him during illness, old age, or fear.

This is not actually Keiko. It is the role of being cared for, remembered through
him. It protects the husband because that is what care means to it.

Combat behavior:

- Interposes between attacks and the husband.
- Restores or steadies other memories.
- Pulls the husband away from danger and toward the complaint.
- Tries to soothe PCs with phrases that sound almost kind.

Possible lines:

- "He needs her."
- "Do not make him go alone."
- "She promised."
- "Let him hold on."

When defeated:

It remembers one concrete detail: the shape of Keiko's hand, a hospital room, a
blanket, a cup of tea, or a pill organizer. Then it fades.

## Encounter Objective

The PCs are not trying to kill enemies.

They are trying to keep the husband from reaching and overloading the complaint
until the room stabilizes.

Suggested objective:

> Keep the complaint intact for five rounds, or until all manifested memories are
> dispersed.

The husband advances slowly toward the complaint each round unless stopped,
blocked, spoken to, or physically restrained.

The memories try to clear his path.

## Stakes

If the husband reaches the complaint, choose one outcome:

### Softer Failure

The husband touches the complaint. His face becomes clear. He remembers Keiko's
name and says it once. Then the complaint loses key details: the complainant
field, the phone number, or the complaint number. The party keeps partial clues,
but the old woman's trace is weaker.

### Harder Failure

The husband fully attaches to the complaint. For a few seconds he is real enough
to understand that Keiko is gone. Then the complaint collapses into blank, damp
paper. The party may retain only what they copied, photographed, or personally
remembered.

### Bittersweet Success

The party stops him, but before he fades he remembers one true thing about Keiko.
This memory becomes a reward clue or emotional anchor.

Examples:

- Her name.
- The nickname he used for her.
- A phrase she said before making the Town Hall complaint.
- A detail from a seaside walk where the third span of the north wall was visible.
- The fact that she kept calling because the pavement was wet even on clear days.

## PC Awakening Beats

Use this encounter to let the PCs access spells for the first time.

Do not frame this as leveling up. Frame it as reality becoming thin enough that
their existing entanglements can act.

### Alex Gaga

Alex sees the complaint, the paper fibers, the water, the cabinet labels, and the
room's civic infrastructure as a system with rhythm.

The pearl bracelet from [[Actors/Mollusk|Mollusk]] tightens or warms.

Possible awakening expression:

- She reinforces the complaint by matching the room's rhythm.
- She disrupts a memory's advance with sound, command, or pressure.
- She heals or steadies someone by reestablishing their place in the pattern.

Table-facing line:

> You do not understand the spell. You understand the room.

### Vincent Uminashi

Vincent recognizes that this is not just evidence. It is inheritance, continuity,
and the danger of being the last person who remembers.

Possible awakening expression:

- He speaks with inherited authority.
- He places himself in the correct spot without knowing why.
- He recognizes the difference between a true memory and a role trying to become
  real.

Table-facing line:

> Your family has done something like this before. You do not know when. Your body
> knows where to stand.

### Ryunnu

Ryunnu receives an impossible administrative prompt through the Mayor's mistaken
patron interface.

Possible prompt:

> UNAUTHORIZED MEMORY ANCHOR DETECTED.
> TEMPORARY PRESERVATION AUTHORITY AVAILABLE.
> ACCEPT? Y/N

Possible awakening expression:

- He denies a memory's access to the complaint.
- He shields the complaint through accidental civic-divine authority.
- He casts by selecting an option that should not exist.

The Mayor should react with alarm, not exposition.

Possible Mayor line:

> That was not intended.

### Aizawa Marin

Marin feels the same deep-sea familiarity from the cafe rift, but now it is
wrapped around human grief.

Possible awakening expression:

- She senses which memory will move next.
- She redirects water, pressure, or cold.
- She recognizes that the complaint is not Keiko, even though the memories want it
  to be.

Table-facing line:

> Whatever waited beyond the rift recognizes this grief. It recognizes you too.

## Fight Logistics

Use these mechanics if the scene needs to run like a concrete D&D encounter rather
than a freeform skill challenge.

The encounter has three active layers:

1. The complaint as the protected objective.
2. The husband as the emotional pressure moving toward it.
3. The room as an unstable battlefield that keeps reconstructing his life badly.

The fight should begin when the complaint has just been stabilized and the room
is suddenly covered by two inches of seawater.

## How To Tell Players What To Do

Do not announce a hidden subsystem first. Teach the rule through visible feedback.

When a PC copies the complaint number, photographs the page, says Keiko's name, or
otherwise preserves a detail, describe the original complaint briefly stabilizing.

Example:

> As you write the complaint number into your notebook, the ink on the original
> stops crawling for half a second. Not because the copy is magical. Because there
> are now two versions.

This teaches the table:

> The way to protect the complaint is to remember it in more places.

Good preservation actions include:

- reading the name, number, or complaint text aloud
- copying the text by hand
- photographing or texting it
- memorizing a detail and telling another PC to remember it too
- sketching the shoreline-like ink pattern
- placing the paper in a folder, sleeve, bag, or under glass
- casting a spell that protects, clarifies, stabilizes, reveals, or anchors
- invoking civic authority, family authority, divine authority, or emotional
  attachment

Let almost any sincere attempt help if it creates another record, witness, or
anchor.

## Complaint Integrity And Anchors

Track the complaint with a simple clock.

Suggested starting value:

> Complaint Integrity 8

The complaint loses Integrity when:

- the husband reaches it: -3 Integrity
- a manifested memory reaches it: -2 Integrity
- a room event directly hits it: -1 Integrity
- a PC carrying it is knocked prone, submerged, or forced to drop it: -1 Integrity

The complaint gains or protects Integrity when PCs create Anchors.

An Anchor is any new place where the complaint's meaning now exists. Examples:

- a photo of the complaint
- a handwritten copy
- a spoken name repeated by another PC
- a text message with the complaint number
- a sketch of the shoreline ink pattern
- a PC explicitly committing a detail to memory

Each Anchor can do one of the following:

- restore 1 Integrity
- prevent the next 1 Integrity loss
- give advantage on one check to stabilize, read, or protect the complaint

Limit each distinct method to one Anchor unless the table invents a meaningfully
new version. Four players all taking identical phone photos is less interesting
than one photo, one handwritten copy, one spoken witness, and one memorized name.

## Round Structure

At the start of combat:

- The room gains two inches of seawater.
- The complaint remains dry at the center of the scene.
- The husband appears at the edge of the room.
- One or two memories manifest between him and the party.

At initiative count 20, run a Room Memory Event.

At the end of each round, the seawater rises by another two inches unless the
party has clearly stabilized the room or you want a shorter fight.

Suggested fight length:

- 5 rounds, or
- until all active memories are dispersed, or
- until the husband chooses to stop reaching for the complaint.

## Rising Water

The water is not meant to drown the PCs. It is meant to make the room feel less
like a normal records office every round.

### 2 inches

The fight begins here.

The floor is wet. Paper edges darken. Sound becomes slightly muffled. Footprints
fill immediately with brine.

No mechanical penalty.

### 4 inches

Loose papers, folders, pens, and dropped items begin to float.

Any unattended loose evidence drifts 5 feet at initiative count 20 unless pinned,
held, or anchored.

### 6 inches

The room edges become difficult terrain as water gathers around cabinets,
furniture, and manifested objects.

A creature knocked prone must use 5 extra feet of movement to stand unless helped.

### 8 inches

Electrical systems become dangerous.

When a light fixture, computer, outlet, printer, or exposed wire is disturbed,
nearby creatures make a Dexterity save or take minor lightning damage. Keep this
small; the point is pressure, not lethality.

Ryunnu should get useful sensory feedback here: error tones, municipal warning
lights, emergency prompts, or impossible circuit labels.

### 12 inches

The room is becoming unsustainable.

All ground-level paper objects are at risk. Movement through cluttered areas is
difficult terrain. The next time the complaint takes damage, describe the paper
buckling as if it has been wet for years.

This is a good point to end the fight, force a hard choice, or have the husband
understand that the complaint cannot carry him.

## Room Memory Events

Use one event each round at initiative count 20. Pick what fits the pacing or roll
1d6.

### 1. Workplace Cabinets

The husband remembers work, but not clearly.

Rows of office filing cabinets slide into the room at wrong angles. Some are Town
Hall cabinets. Some are from his old workplace. Some are impossible mixtures of
both.

Effect:

- Place two cabinet clusters as half-cover obstacles.
- Each cluster slides 10 feet in a straight line.
- Creatures in the path make a Dexterity save or are shoved 10 feet and knocked
  prone.
- A cabinet cluster can be attacked or shoved over to create difficult terrain
  instead of cover.

Table text:

> The cabinets do not open. They commute.

### 2. Office Day That Never Ended

A desk, calendar, time card, and fluorescent clock appear.

The clock has no hands. The calendar has every weekday printed as tomorrow.

Effect:

- One PC within the area makes a Wisdom save.
- On a failure, they lose their reaction until the next round as routine tries to
  settle over them.
- If another PC says the old woman's name or reminds them why they are here, the
  effect ends immediately.

### 3. Commuter Lane

The husband remembers going home.

A train platform line or crosswalk stripe cuts across the room. Faceless commuters
move through it, all carrying damp briefcases or grocery bags.

Effect:

- Create a 10-foot-wide lane across the battlefield.
- The lane is difficult terrain.
- The first time a creature enters it each round, it makes a Strength or Dexterity
  save or is pushed 10 feet along the lane.

This can separate PCs without making the commuters enemies.

### 4. Hospital Curtain

The husband remembers illness and care.

Hospital curtains unfurl from nowhere. A rolling bed frame drifts through the
water. IV tubing loops around chair legs and ankles.

Effect:

- Create a lightly obscured zone.
- One creature in the zone makes a Dexterity save or is restrained by tubing until
  it uses an action to escape.
- The Caretaker memory gains temporary hit points or advantage on its next attempt
  to protect the husband.

### 5. Wedding Dance

The husband remembers a vow more strongly than he remembers the date.

A dance floor appears under the water. Music plays as if heard through an old
speaker in another room.

Effect:

- Creatures within 15 feet of the husband make a Wisdom save.
- On a failure, they are pulled 10 feet toward the husband or the complaint, their
  choice of which feels worse.
- A PC who speaks a concrete remembered detail can grant advantage to another PC's
  save.

### 6. The Complaint Rings

The complaint number rings like an old phone.

Effect:

- Complaint loses 1 Integrity unless at least one PC uses their reaction to answer,
  cover, recite, copy, or otherwise attend to it.
- If a PC answers the ringing, give them a fragment instead of damage.

Possible fragments:

- "Third span, north wall."
- "It's wet even when the weather is clear."
- "Please tell someone before a child falls."
- "Keiko, don't forget your bag."

## Memory Stat Block Guidance

Use simple low-level stat blocks and reskin them. Do not overbuild.

The party should feel danger, but the scene's real tension is objective pressure,
not a lethal boss fight.

### Memory Dog

Base: wolf or mastiff.

Changes:

- Remove Pack Tactics if using wolf.
- Add Reunion Instinct: as a bonus action, the dog can Dash toward the husband or
  complaint.
- Replace some bite attempts with Shove attempts.

Tactics:

- Knock PCs prone.
- Open paths.
- Grab loose evidence.
- Do not fight to the death; disperse when reduced to 0 HP.

### Younger Husband Memory

Base: thug, bandit captain, or veteran depending on party strength.

Reskin weapon attacks as desperate grapples, shoulder checks, and impossible
persistence.

Optional feature:

> Hold On. When the younger husband hits a creature, he may attempt to grapple it
> instead of dealing full damage.

Tactics:

- Grapple blockers.
- Drag them away from the husband's path.
- Stand between the older husband and anyone trying to restrain him.

### Caretaker Memory

Base: priest, acolyte, or cult fanatic depending on party strength.

Reskin healing as memory reinforcement.

Optional features:

- Interpose: reaction to impose disadvantage on one attack against the husband.
- Soothe: one creature makes a Wisdom save or cannot make opportunity attacks
  against the husband until the end of its next turn.

Tactics:

- Protect the husband.
- Stabilize other memories.
- Make the PCs feel like they are the cruel ones for blocking him.

### Workplace Memory Object

Base: animated object, animated armor, or custom hazard.

Use this when you want something clearly physical to hit that is not a person or
animal.

Examples:

- filing cabinet cluster
- rolling desk
- stamping machine
- time clock
- office chair with too many wheels

Suggested properties:

- AC: low to moderate
- HP: low enough to destroy in one or two focused hits
- Damage: shove, restrain, or pin rather than high damage

Tactics:

- Block lines of movement.
- Pin PCs against cabinets.
- Separate a protector from the complaint.
- Make the room feel like it is assembling the wrong life from the wrong records.

## Husband Movement

The older husband should not feel like a boss monster.

Run him as a slow objective pressure.

At the end of his turn:

- If unblocked, he moves 10 feet toward the complaint.
- If blocked by a PC, he asks to pass before trying to move around them.
- If physically restrained, he does not attack unless you need a shove to keep the
  scene moving.
- If spoken to with a true detail about Keiko, he pauses for one round.
- If shown that the complaint is not Keiko, he may hesitate or begin to understand.

Useful things that can pause him:

- saying Keiko's full name
- repeating a detail from her phone
- telling him she was trying to protect children near the seawall
- showing him a photo from her phone
- promising to carry his memory without letting him consume the complaint

## Victory Conditions

The encounter can end in several ways.

### Tactical Victory

The PCs disperse all active memories and keep the husband from reaching the
complaint for five rounds.

### Emotional Victory

The PCs convince the husband that the complaint is not Keiko and cannot bring her
back.

He stops reaching for it voluntarily.

### Preservation Victory

The complaint would have failed, but the PCs created enough Anchors that its key
information survives in them, on their phones, in notes, or through spoken memory.

### Partial Failure

The husband reaches the complaint, but the PCs preserve enough secondary Anchors
to keep the investigation moving.

Do not hard-stop the campaign because of failed mechanics. Let failure cost
clarity, emotional comfort, or exact details rather than the entire lead.

## Table-Facing Clarity

Use plain language once initiative begins. The players should understand the
objective even if the characters do not understand the metaphysics.

Good framing line:

> The complaint is the only stable thing in the room. The husband and the memories
> are all trying to reach it. Every time something touches it, the ink buckles and
> the name gets weaker. What do you do?

If players seem stuck, let Ryunnu receive a Mayor-channel hint:

> PRESERVATION IMPROVES WITH REDUNDANT RECORDS.

Or, less bureaucratic:

> Do not let it remain singular.

## Running The Tone

Keep the memories active and dangerous, but not evil.

They can hurt the PCs. They can shove, bite, grapple, shield, and strike. But the
emotional text should stay desperate rather than predatory.

Avoid:

- crab monsters
- sea wolves
- generic drowned spirits
- a villain speech
- making the husband the boss

Prefer:

- the dog trying to reunite its people
- the younger husband trying to keep a vow
- the caretaker trying to protect someone who is already gone
- attacks that feel like misplaced love, panic, or devotion

## Ending The Encounter

When the room stabilizes, the water disappears.

The filing cabinets are dry. The lights are ordinary. The complaint is just paper
again.

But something remains changed:

- The complaint still contains at least one preserved detail.
- The PCs know they can do impossible things.
- The party understands that remembering is not passive; it can attract pressure.
- The old woman's absence now has emotional weight beyond the investigation.

Possible final image:

The husband fades last. He looks at the complaint, then at the PCs.

He does not ask them to save him.

He says:

> "Tell her I remembered."

Then he is gone, and the only proof he existed is what the PCs choose to carry
forward.
