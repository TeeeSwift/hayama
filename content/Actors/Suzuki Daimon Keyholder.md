# Suzuki Daimon Keyholder

## Role

A Suzuki-linked Daimon operator who controls access through land, keys,
deliveries, favors, and old local arrangements.

This character should be named when they become active at the table. Until then,
use this note as the functional slot for Daimon's first major face.

## Location

Primary location: [[Chronicles/Campaign Prep/Drifted Families and Daimon|Daimon]],
a local liquor store with hidden access power.

They may also appear anywhere Daimon has access:

- delivery alleys
- festival storage rooms
- construction gates
- shrine-adjacent sheds
- private roads
- old storehouses
- municipal back entrances

Their home base should feel ordinary: the shop counter, delivery van, back room,
or storage entrance.

## Faction

- [[Actors/The Drifted|The Drifted]], through Suzuki family lineage
- [[Chronicles/Campaign Prep/Drifted Families and Daimon|Daimon]], through land,
  logistics, and access
- Suzuki-linked rather than openly factional

Publicly, they are a liquor store employee, proprietor, delivery coordinator, or
family operator.

Privately, they are a Suzuki-line access broker who inherited partial
threshold-oriented divinity.

## Narrative Function

They provide access.

Use this character when the players need to know:

- how to get into a locked construction site
- who had access to a restricted area
- which old family is meeting quietly
- which parcel of land actually belongs to whom
- how to move something through town unnoticed
- how to get somewhere without going through official channels

They are not a heroic quest-giver. They are a fixer.

Their help should be useful and slightly compromising.

## What They Represent

They represent adaptation as power.

Where the Tsunodas preserve memory and grievance, this character treats imperfect
inheritance as something to use.

They embody:

- access over title
- favors as infrastructure
- survival through compromise
- threshold power hidden inside ordinary logistics
- the idea that the person with the key may matter more than the person with the
  authority

Their implied philosophy:

> The world changed. We learned the new routes.

## What They Provide The Story

### Practical Utility

They can:

- open doors
- reveal property ownership
- get the party near restricted sites
- overhear faction movement
- move objects through town
- provide mundane cover
- connect the party to neutral locals

### Social Complication

Every favor creates a debt.

The debt does not have to be sinister, but it should be real.

Examples:

- "Take this crate with you."
- "Don't mention how you got in."
- "If anyone asks, you were buying festival sake."
- "Tell the Tsunodas we still remember what they owe."
- "Bring back whatever you find behind that door."

### Thematic Pressure

They force the party to ask:

> Is using a compromised system different from being compromised by it?

## Partial Powers

Their powers should remain grounded and deniable at first.

Possible expressions:

- knows when someone crosses a threshold Daimon controls
- can sense whether someone has permission to be somewhere, even if the law says
  otherwise
- finds service routes, side gates, and forgotten access points
- makes small bargains stick unnaturally well
- can pass unnoticed where Daimon has been invited
- can turn hospitality into temporary protection
- recognizes old access agreements embedded in land, shrine, business, or family
  custom

## Relationship Hooks

### [[Actors/Player Characters/Alex Gaga|Alex Gaga]]

They may know land and project records that complicate Alex's shoreline approval.
They can reveal that the project crossed more old boundaries than the municipal
paperwork admits.

### [[Actors/Player Characters/Vincent Uminashi|Vincent Uminashi]]

They may know old access arrangements tied to Vincent's family better than
Vincent does.

### [[Actors/Player Characters/Ryunnu|Ryunnu]]

Ryunnu's anomalous registration may recognize this character as authorized in
places where they have no visible official right to be.

### [[Actors/Player Characters/Aizawa Marin|Aizawa Marin]]

Marin may sense their Drifted power as controlled, practical, and threshold-bound
rather than wounded in the same way as hers.

## First Scene

The party needs access to the shoreline stabilization site after hours.

Someone says:

> Ask Daimon. They've got a key.

At Daimon, this character does not ask why the party needs it. They already know
enough.

They ask:

> Front gate, side gate, or the one the city forgot it built?

## Voice

Calm, practical, lightly amused. Never impressed by locked doors.

They speak like someone who has already checked the exits.
