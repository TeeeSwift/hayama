# Horizon

## Role

Horizon is an Anchored concept for distance, promise, limit, and the line that
recedes as one approaches it.

Use this concept for an Anchored connected to longing, navigation, and deferred
answers.

## Natural Model

The horizon is the apparent boundary where sea and sky meet. It is not a place
one can reach by moving toward it. It depends on position, height, atmosphere,
and curvature.

It orients travelers while remaining unreachable.

This makes Horizon a figure of:

- guidance without arrival
- longing structured as direction
- the edge of knowledge
- promises that move as people move
- perspective determining boundary

## Campaign Use

Horizon should represent goals that guide the party even when they cannot be
completed in the expected way.

They may know about old voyages, impossible returns, things seen offshore, or
truths that become clearer only from a different height.

Use Horizon when the campaign needs:

- a distant answer that changes as the party approaches
- a character who offers orientation rather than closure
- a reminder that some boundaries are perspective, not walls

## Table Motifs

- looking from rooftops, hills, seawalls, and boats
- weather changing what can be seen
- maps with blank outer edges
- promises made toward the sea
- a person who never points at the object, only at the direction
