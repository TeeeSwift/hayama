# Driftwood

## Role

Driftwood is an Anchored concept for uprooted things that survive by becoming
useful somewhere else.

Use this concept for someone detached from origin, family, or place, but not
destroyed by that loss.

## Natural Model

Driftwood is wood carried by rivers, tides, storms, and currents. By the time it
returns to shore, it has been stripped of bark, softened by water, bleached by
sun, and made lighter than its original form.

It can become shelter, fuel, art, debris, or a navigational sign.

This makes Driftwood a figure of:

- displacement
- survival after uprooting
- usefulness without belonging
- messages carried by currents
- the dignity of weathered remains

## Campaign Use

Driftwood should represent a life after severance.

They may know what it means to be carried by forces larger than oneself. They
may help the party read where something came from by studying what the tide left
behind.

Use Driftwood when the campaign needs:

- an Anchored who understands exile
- evidence transported from one site to another
- a quiet critique of roots, bloodlines, and ownership

## Table Motifs

- pale wood wedged in a drain or gate
- a walking stick collected from the shore
- maps based on where debris accumulates
- objects that have traveled farther than people believe
- a person who never sits with their back fully to the sea
