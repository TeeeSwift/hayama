# Uminashi Seawall

The Uminashi seawall is a recent shoreline stabilization project tied to the
current crisis.

The problem is not only structural. Beneath the seawall is a divine medallion or
medallion-like piece of [[Lore/The Barrier|The Barrier]] that requires the rhythm
of incoming waves to stay aligned. By changing how the sea strikes and withdraws
from the shore, the seawall has disrupted the anchor point's timing and caused a
Barrier failure.

The wall does not need to have been built incorrectly. It may have passed
ordinary inspection and still become dangerous afterward through erosion,
foundation exposure, and the deeper rhythm failure that ordinary engineering
records cannot describe.

The significant erosion at the third span is real, and the Coast Guard scan
confirmed exposed foundation. A third-party structural audit may still conclude
that the wall is within tolerance: concerning, strange, and worth monitoring,
but not yet an immediate collapse risk or clear construction defect.

## Current State

- Completed roughly two months before [[Chronicles/Session 1/index|Session 1]].
- Publicly justified as erosion control and long-term coastal protection.
- The third span of the north wall has an exposed foundation.
- [[Actors/Hayashi|Hayashi]] has requested a third-party structural audit.
- [[Actors/Player Characters/Vincent Uminashi|Vincent Uminashi]] asked
  [[Actors/Chizuru|Chizuru]] who handled the construction project.
- [[Actors/The Anchored|The Anchored]] did not realize the wall would disrupt the
  Barrier. Bringing the wall down or redesigning it is the crude, reliable fix,
  but they may also be testing ways to stabilize the anchor in place or relocate
  it through old access paths.
- The [[Actors/The Anchored|Anchored]] may have tried accelerating or exploiting
  the erosion, then realized that making the wall structurally indefensible
  would require damage so extreme it would look like obvious sabotage.

## Anchor Access And Options

The anchor is not sealed away forever. Old maintenance and ritual routes may
still reach it: drainage paths, shrine-adjacent passages, Daimon/Suzuki access
routes, coastal service corridors, and property boundaries that do not match
modern maps.

One working possibility is that these routes lead to an
[[Lore/WIP Underwater Cave Shrine|underwater cave shrine]]: a hidden sacred
infrastructure site that may house the medallion, connect to the Relief Chamber,
or function as a communing point with [[Actors/Watatsumi|Watatsumi]].

The Anchored have several possible strategies:

- remove or redesign the wall, restoring the old wave rhythm as directly as
  possible
- stabilize the anchor in place with patches, false anchors, resonance marks,
  redirected water, or temporary containment
- relocate the medallion through the old access network, then recalibrate the
  surrounding Barrier

Wall removal is the most reliable known answer, but it is not the only
conceivable answer. Relocation or in-place stabilization creates more room for
faction disagreement, old access debts, and player choice.

## Anchored Pressure Campaign

The Anchored's first instinct may have been physical: weaken the wall enough
that [[Atlas/Hayama|Hayama]] would have to remove or redesign it. The wall is too
competent for that to work cleanly. To force a structural failure quickly, they
would have to dig or damage an unnatural amount, risking exposure and public
panic.

Their likely pivot is political, legal, financial, and social pressure:

- preserve or leak evidence that makes the wall look risky
- make post-completion maintenance harder to ignore
- encourage complaints and resident concern
- expose access irregularities
- make insurers, officials, and old families nervous
- chip away at the wall's credibility and value until removal or redesign
  becomes the least costly option

This makes their behavior suspicious without making them simple vandals. They
may be right that the wall has to change, but their method is manipulation of
public trust.

## Campaign Use

The seawall is the first civic/infrastructure lead pointing toward the damaged
[[Lore/The Barrier|Barrier]] anchor point.

The useful misdirection is that the wall looks like a public-works defect or a
liability problem. The deeper issue is rhythmic: the wall is protecting the
shore in exactly the way that prevents the Barrier from hearing the sea
correctly.
