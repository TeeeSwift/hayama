# Nagi

## Role

Nagi is an Anchored concept for calm seas, suspended motion, and the unsettling
quiet before or after force.

Use this concept for an Anchored whose gentleness is not peace so much as
temporary absence of visible movement.

## Natural Model

Nagi refers to calm conditions, especially calm sea or wind. Calm water can be
beautiful, safe, and reflective, but it can also hide depth, pressure, and the
fact that motion has not ended, only quieted.

Calm is a condition, not a guarantee.

This makes Nagi a figure of:

- stillness as active state
- quiet that may be mercy or warning
- reflection made possible by lack of disturbance
- the danger of mistaking calm for resolution
- emotional control under pressure

## Campaign Use

Nagi should represent de-escalation that may or may not be honest.

They may calm crowds, suppress panic, settle supernatural pressure, or make a
room feel safe enough for truth to surface. They may also delay action too long.

Use Nagi when the campaign needs:

- a peaceful scene with unease underneath
- a character who lowers temperature without solving cause
- a reminder that quiet can be engineered

## Table Motifs

- glassy water
- sounds becoming suddenly clear
- a person speaking softly enough that others match them
- rooms where anger drains away too quickly
- the question "is this peace, or just a pause?"
