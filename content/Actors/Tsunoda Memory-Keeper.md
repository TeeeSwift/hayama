# Tsunoda Memory-Keeper

## Role

A Tsunoda family archivist, ritualist, genealogist, or elder who preserves
memory, names, and inherited divine lineage against erosion and reaping.

This character should be named when they become active at the table. Until then,
use this note as the functional slot for the Tsunoda family's first major face.

## Location

A place rooted in preserved memory:

- an old family house
- a shrine-adjacent archive
- a seaside cemetery
- a room filled with handwritten genealogies
- a tea room overlooking an older coastline
- a storehouse containing ritual objects and salt-stained records

The location should feel old, careful, and resistant to change.

## Faction

- Tsunoda Drifted lineage
- [[Actors/The Drifted|The Drifted]], conditionally and philosophically rather
  than politically

Publicly, this character may appear to be:

- a local historian
- genealogist
- calligrapher
- archivist
- teacher
- ritual caretaker

Privately, they are the keeper of a damaged divine inheritance.

## Narrative Function

They provide counter-history.

Use this character when the players need to ask:

- what the Anchored did
- whether this has happened before
- why some people remember what others forget
- what Marin is carrying
- how divinity drifts without disappearing entirely
- what families lost when Hayama was stabilized

They should not provide objective truth. They provide remembered truth.

That truth should be emotionally convincing, historically incomplete, and shaped
by inherited grievance.

## What They Represent

They represent memory as resistance.

Their worldview:

> If we forget the shape of what was taken, then it has been taken twice.

They embody:

- inherited grievance
- sacred recordkeeping
- continuity across generations
- refusal to let systems overwrite harm
- the dignity and danger of remembering too much

They are what happens when a wound becomes genealogy.

## What They Provide The Story

### Lore With Bias

They can explain:

- old divine lineages
- names of lost powers
- why reaping distorts memory
- how divinity survives displacement
- why some families hate the Barrier
- why Drifted lineages preserved certain rituals

But their explanations should not be neutral.

They may:

- over-attribute harm to the Anchored
- understate why the Barrier was built
- treat restoration as morally obvious
- frame compromise as surrender

### Protection Against Memory Loss

They can provide:

- a name-preserving charm
- rituals that allow the party to remember partially reaped people
- records that resist revision
- warnings about names spoken near water
- ways to identify displaced divinity

### Pressure On Marin

This character is especially useful for
[[Actors/Player Characters/Aizawa Marin|Aizawa Marin]].

Possible lines:

> That is not yours. But it has learned your shape.

Or:

> You are not the first vessel to survive a mistake.

They give Marin's condition historical and social context rather than treating it
as a singular anomaly.

## Partial Powers

Their power should feel ritualized, inherited, and costly.

Possible expressions:

- sensing divine residue in people or places
- preserving names through ritual recitation
- resisting memory softening during reaping events
- binding a small manifestation briefly by naming what it used to be
- reading records that outsiders cannot fully perceive
- recognizing the source of displaced blessings
- calling back fragments of ancestral divinity temporarily

## Relationship Hooks

### [[Actors/Player Characters/Aizawa Marin|Aizawa Marin]]

May see Marin as:

- proof that divinity survives displacement
- a warning
- an heir
- a stolen inheritance
- a possible continuation of Drifted survival

### [[Actors/Player Characters/Vincent Uminashi|Vincent Uminashi]]

May know Vincent's family history and ask what his lineage chose to forget.

### [[Actors/Player Characters/Alex Gaga|Alex Gaga]]

May interpret Alex's shoreline project involvement as another example of
institutions damaging sacred systems without understanding them.

### [[Actors/Player Characters/Ryunnu|Ryunnu]]

May recognize Ryunnu's anomaly as a name filed into the wrong system.

## First Scene

After a reaping event, an NPC's name begins disappearing from local memory.

The party is sent to the Tsunoda memory-keeper.

The memory-keeper asks them to write the missing person's name three times:

1. once as the party remembers it
2. once as the sea remembers it
3. once as the town refuses to remember it

Only one version remains legible.

## Voice

Measured, precise, emotionally controlled.

They speak like every word should survive being forgotten.
